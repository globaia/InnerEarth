About InnerEarth

You've spent your whole life on Earth. Now step inside it.

InnerEarth inverts the globe. Instead of gazing down at a spinning marble from some impossible orbital perch, you stand at the center—your center, wherever you are—while the entire planet curves around you like the interior of a vast sphere. Logarithmic scaling lets your street and your hemisphere coexist in the same view, each legible, each connected.

This sounds new. It is. And yet.

Humans have been dreaming toward this moment for millennia. The Greek agora where all could see and be seen. Boullée's impossible hollow sphere designed to contain the cosmos. Escher's hand holding a mirrored ball that held him back. The curved habitats of science fiction—O'Neill cylinders, Rama, Cooper Station—where the world wraps upward into sky. The cosmic eggs of a dozen creation myths. The planetarium inverted: not stars projected above, but home projected around.

InnerEarth is where these streams converge.

What you'll feel is double: the thrill of genuine novelty, and the strange recognition of something your imagination has always been reaching for. The unfamiliar will feel familiar. This is not a bug—it's the reason it works.

We are not on Earth. We are of it, within it, held by it. InnerEarth makes that truth visible.
